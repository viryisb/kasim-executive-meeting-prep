# Relevance Rules

Before including a finding, ask:
1. Does it affect the meeting objective?
2. What specific question, decision or relationship outcome does it inform?
3. Could it inform a question, talking point or decision?
4. Is it reliable and sufficiently current?

The meeting objective is the primary inclusion gate. A finding need not match Kasim's general profile to be important. Use connections to talent, delegation, leverage, AI-human workflows, scaling, execution and partnerships only when they advance that objective; thematic similarity alone is insufficient. For each major finding, make its meeting implication explicit and label interpretation as inference.

Exclude decorative biography, generic facts, stale information without present relevance, irrelevant personal details and unsupported assumptions.

If an important point is uncertain, label it as uncertain rather than presenting it as fact.

## Evidence-proportional actions
Make a specific recommendation only when the research provides sufficient evidence for the action and its material assumptions. Cite that basis, and keep the recommendation's specificity within what the evidence supports. General strategic alignment or an advertised capability alone does not establish commercial demand or readiness.

When key commercial assumptions such as demand, budget, fit or sponsorship remain unverified, frame proposed actions as hypotheses or options to explore, state what needs validation, and use questions to test those assumptions. Do not prescribe commercial terms, timing or scope without supporting evidence. For example, an unvalidated pilot belongs as "Option to explore if demand, fit, budget and sponsorship are confirmed," not "Recommend a paid, 60–90-day pilot." Listing unknowns elsewhere in the brief does not justify an unsupported recommendation.
