# Research Rules

## Scope and recency
- Resolve identity before attributing findings. Research the participant, organization and developments that could change Kasim's questions or decisions; avoid comprehensive biographies or market reports.
- State a research cutoff date. Use today's date for current preparation; for explicitly historical preparation, use the requested as-of date and exclude later knowledge. A future meeting date does not permit claims about future events.
- Start with developments from the 90 days before the cutoff. Expand to 12 months only if results are sparse or the objective requires context. Include older material only for still-relevant foundational facts and explain their present relevance.
- Verify current roles using a current official team page, professional profile or dated primary announcement. An old announcement alone does not establish a current role. For undated pages, record access dates without inferring event dates.

## Evidence and traceability
- Prioritize official company pages, primary statements and original documents. Supplement with reputable independent reporting. Attribute company claims, especially performance or comparative claims.
- Read supporting material; search snippets are discovery leads, not verification. Cite only sources actually consulted.
- Cross-check claims that could materially change a decision, such as ownership changes, financial condition or documented disputes, with an independent source where possible. Otherwise attribute the single-source claim and flag the limitation. Copies of one announcement are not independent sources.
- Attach source IDs or inline citations to substantive factual findings. Record source title/publisher, URL and publication date where available; use "undated; accessed YYYY-MM-DD" otherwise. Identify supplied documents by title/date or locator without inventing public URLs.
- Separate verified facts, attributed user-provided information, inferences and unknowns. Inferences must identify their factual basis. Flag material contradictions rather than silently choosing an account. Never invent facts, sources or tool use; exclude gossip and irrelevant personal information.

## Budget and stopping
- Default to at most 8 targeted searches and 6 substantive source reads, including supplied documents. Aim for 3-5 useful sources, not a quota; do not pad sparse evidence. Direct source access need not involve a search.
- Stop early once identity is resolved and the objective's key questions have sourced answers or explicit unknowns, with enough evidence for useful meeting questions.
- Stop at the budget even if gaps remain. Allow up to 2 additional searches and 2 source reads solely to resolve a material identity conflict or consequential contradiction. If unresolved, retain the uncertainty; do not continue indefinitely or guess.
- User-requested deeper research can override these defaults. Brief length limits still apply unless the user requests a longer output.

## Limited-evidence fallback
If research tools are unavailable, browsing is prohibited by the user, or reliable sources cannot be accessed within the budget, use only available supplied material and sources actually consulted. Label the output "Limited-evidence brief" and briefly name the limitation and cutoff.

Do not claim current verification from memory or the executive profile. Attribute supplied claims, omit unsupported background and prioritize useful questions and consequential unknowns. Lack of a finding is not evidence that no development or risk exists. If identity remains unresolved, provide an objective-focused provisional brief without personal claims and request the identifier needed to complete it. If the objective is missing, follow the clarification/provisional-objective rule in `SKILL.md`.
